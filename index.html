Vision-Language Guided Online Domain Selection and Enrichment for PDDL-based Robot Manipulation

Anonymous authors

Code available here
Supplementary video Simulation results Real-world results

Abstract

Natural-language robot manipulation requires converting user instructions and visual observations into plans that are semantically meaningful and executable. Direct foundation-model planning is flexible but difficult to verify, while symbolic planning provides explicit action semantics but requires manually engineered domains. We propose a fully local framework that bridges these approaches, assuming the availability of general-purpose, reusable PDDL domains. Given the natural-language task, the framework selects a reusable domain, identifies missing symbolic knowledge, and introduces symbolic extensions constrained to available robot skills. A grounded planning problem is then constructed and solved by a standard PDDL planner. During execution, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) selects controller-specific parameters without modifying the symbolic action semantics. We evaluate the framework in simulated and real-world manipulation scenarios, comparing it with alternative baselines and across language models of different sizes.
